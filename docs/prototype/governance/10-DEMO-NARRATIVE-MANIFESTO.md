# HIKARI Prototype — Demo Narrative Manifesto

## 1. Purpose

The demonstration is a guided industrial story. Navigation is subordinate to narrative.

## 2. Target duration

The executive demonstration should fit approximately 10–15 minutes, with deeper exploration available only if discussion requires it.

## 3. Narrative arc

### Opening — Why HIKARI
Establish the two executive outcomes: operational efficiency visibility and production predictability.

### Step 1 — What do we need to produce?
Show short-term Production Schedule / Plano Hora-Hora, current Productive Area, data freshness, Lots, quantities, Production Order correlation, and demand destination.

### Step 2 — Do we have the conditions to produce?
Show readiness: raw material, Resource eligibility/availability context, Setup context, buffer current/projected coverage, relevant downstream projection.

### Step 3 — Is this the best operational sequence?
Show the planning baseline and a controlled operational resequencing example. Explain that Supervisor and Production Leader can organize execution inside the shift/day while protecting commitments.

### Step 4 — Where will we produce?
Show Dispatching to eligible Resources. Do not imply Balancing selects the machine.

### Step 5 — Execute
Show current Lot, Resource, planned versus produced quantity, operator/context, progress, and conceptual start/pause/confirm/finish actions.

### Step 6 — What happened?
Show WIP/events and a demonstrative interruption. Preserve the relationship between operational event and execution.

### Step 7 — Are we fulfilling the plan?
Show planned/dispatched/actual comparison, adherence, delay/deviation, and bottleneck context.

### Step 8 — How much did we lose and did quality deteriorate?
Show good production, scrap, rework, downtime/performance context, and produced-versus-available distinction.

### Step 9 — Are we efficient?
Show Availability, Performance, Quality and OEE, making the data lineage explicit: execution/events/quality → OEE.

### Step 10 — Will we protect future production?
Show current and projected buffer coverage and downstream demand. Connect Foundry output to Machining and the downstream chain.

### Step 11 — Why modularity matters
Show the eight MES disciplines and Corporate Event Service conceptually. Explain reuse without exposing infrastructure.

### Step 12 — Evolution
Show first-wave capabilities becoming reusable assets for Foundry LP, Machining, Assembly, and future areas.

### Closing — Decision requested
Request validation of the First Wave strategy and authorization to advance to Functional Engineering. Do not request approval of detailed technical architecture.

## 4. Opening screen

Title: **Programa HIKARI — Primeira Onda da Plataforma MES**

Subtitle: **Da programação da produção à visibilidade operacional e OEE**

Label: **Protótipo para alinhamento executivo**

Action: **Iniciar demonstração**

## 5. Guided navigation

The demo must provide Previous/Next and direct access to narrative stages. A possible narrative bar is:

**Planejar → Organizar → Liberar → Executar → Acompanhar → Controlar → Medir**

## 6. Scenario anchor

The reference scenario begins in Foundry, at the beginning of the chain, and demonstrates how planning requirements become Foundry execution and future downstream protection.

## 7. Executive language

Use “proposed”, “reference scenario”, “demonstrative scenario”, “expected impact”, “contributes to mitigation”, and “for validation” where appropriate.

Avoid “final solution”, “closed scope”, “approved architecture”, or claims that a demonstrative metric is real.

## 8. Demonstration discipline

The presenter should not need to explain every field. Each screen must communicate its dominant question within seconds.

## 9. First wireframe checkpoint

Before implementing the full narrative, Wireframe V0.1 must validate the first question: **What do we need to produce?**

Only after this experience is approved should the prototype advance to readiness/rescheduling/dispatching.
