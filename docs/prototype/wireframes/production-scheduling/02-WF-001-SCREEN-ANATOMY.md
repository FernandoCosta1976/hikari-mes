# WF-001 — Screen Anatomy

## 1. Recommended composition

The desktop experience is divided into six visual zones.

### Zone A — Global Shell

Contains:

- HIKARI;
- global Productive Area;
- prototype/demo indication;
- minimal navigation.

This zone must remain visually quiet.

### Zone B — Page Context

Contains:

- title: **Programação da Produção**;
- supporting line: **Plano Hora-Hora**;
- business date/horizon;
- shift selector when relevant;
- freshness indicator.

Suggested microcopy:

**Compromisso de curto prazo recebido do planejamento para organização da execução.**

### Zone C — Commitment Strip

A concise horizontal summary, not a KPI wall.

Possible items:

- Quantidade planejada;
- Lots programados;
- Montagem;
- Reposição/Engenharia where relevant;
- coverage signal;
- attention count.

Avoid more than the decision requires.

### Zone D — Hour-by-Hour Timeline

The dominant zone.

It receives the largest visual area and must immediately communicate the schedule.

### Zone E — Attention / Context Rail

Compact contextual signals:

- buffer;
- raw material;
- reconciliation;
- freshness exceptions.

This can be a side rail or integrated attention strip depending on viewport.

### Zone F — Contextual Detail

Hidden by default.

Opened when a Lot or attention item is selected.

Recommended pattern: right-side drawer/panel on desktop.

## 2. Visual dominance

Approximate conceptual priority:

- Timeline: dominant;
- Context + commitment: secondary;
- Attention: tertiary;
- Detail: on demand.

Do not allocate equal visual weight to every zone.

## 3. Avoid dashboard-grid behavior

The page shall not become a matrix of independent cards.

Information must visually belong to one operational story.

## 4. Suggested reading path

Top-left → context → freshness → commitment → timeline → attention → selected Lot detail.

## 5. Sticky behavior

Recommended:

- global context sticky;
- timeline time scale may remain sticky during vertical scrolling;
- Productive Area must remain visible.

Do not create multiple competing sticky headers.

## 6. Empty margins

Use generous whitespace to separate decisions, not decorative empty space that reduces timeline usability.

## 7. Prototype label

A discreet but persistent indicator:

**Cenário demonstrativo**

It must not compete with the production information.
