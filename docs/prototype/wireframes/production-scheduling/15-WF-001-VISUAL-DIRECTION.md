# WF-001 — Visual Direction

## 1. Reference

The HIKARI prototype shall use the Brazilian T-Systems website as a visual/diagration reference while preserving HIKARI's industrial identity and Yamaha-inspired blue palette.

This is a design-language reference, not permission to copy proprietary assets or reproduce the website.

## 2. Desired qualities

- premium;
- corporate;
- industrial;
- contemporary;
- calm;
- precise;
- information-rich;
- highly legible.

## 3. Composition

Favor:

- strong typographic hierarchy;
- large clean page titles;
- disciplined grids;
- generous white space around major zones;
- dense but organized operational content;
- subtle separators;
- restrained elevation.

## 4. Color

Foundation:

- white;
- light neutral gray;
- dark neutral text;
- deep blue;
- vivid Yamaha-inspired blue.

Priority colors remain governed separately:

- CORE = dark blue;
- ESSENTIAL = light blue;
- IMPORTANT = orange;
- COMPLEMENTARY = green.

Do not use these priority colors indiscriminately in scheduling.

No red anywhere in the prototype.

## 5. Attention

Use orange selectively for attention/risk.

Do not paint entire cards orange when a small marker/message is sufficient.

## 6. Timeline

Timeline should use restrained neutral/blue surfaces.

Selected Lot may receive stronger blue emphasis.

Destination should use badges/text and not depend on color alone.

## 7. Typography

Use a modern sans-serif system consistent with the application stack and licensing constraints.

Do not embed or distribute unauthorized font files.

## 8. Iconography

Simple, line-oriented, semantically clear.

Avoid decorative industrial clip art.

## 9. Cards

Use cards only when they represent a coherent decision object.

Do not wrap every text group in a card.

## 10. Charts

WF-001 is timeline-first.

Do not add charts merely to make the screen appear analytical.

## 11. Brand restraint

The application should feel inspired by corporate design discipline, not like a marketing website pasted onto an MES.

## 12. Prototype presentation

The screen must look credible enough that the Director can imagine it as a future product, while all demonstrative data remains clearly identified.
